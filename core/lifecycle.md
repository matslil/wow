# Lifecycle

The lifecycle stages are Exploration, Concept, Development, Production and
Transition, Utilization, Support and Evolution, and Retirement.

Stages describe system maturity, not a waterfall. They may overlap, repeat, and
apply independently to a product, release, capability, version, or component.
Operational evidence may trigger new Exploration or Concept work. A system may
support one version while retiring another.

Exploration must establish whether a problem or opportunity merits investment.
Concept must compare viable responses and recommend a concept. Development must
define, realize, integrate, verify, and validate a system increment. Production
and Transition must create a repeatable release and introduce it safely.
Utilization must observe whether use produces intended outcomes. Support and
Evolution must sustain and adapt capability. Retirement must withdraw obligations
and preserve, reuse, recycle, or dispose of assets deliberately.

Activities may be shortened only where allowed by starter tailoring. Their
required outcomes must still have evidence or an explicit, approved disposition.
