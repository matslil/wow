# Story Sources and Selection

Reviewed 2026-11-14. These sources offer educational or practitioner judgment,
not comparative proof of a universally best method. We selected actionable ideas
across character, structure, reader expectations, speculative fiction, and agency;
merged overlap and rejected rigid universal prescriptions. The guideline checks
and exceptions are our synthesis. Unverified search snippets were not evidence.

Reuse decision for this Unlicense repository: original synthesis and links only;
no source passages, exercises, examples, art, or source code are imported.
An external source's license does not become the project license. Reassess rights
before any later copying, adaptation, or tool integration; this record is not a
blanket legal clearance.

## S1 — OpenLearn: character creation

[The Open University, character creation](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-1.5)
suggests connecting inner life, behavior, speech, and contradictions. Institutional
introductory teaching; useful for characterization, not evidence that every minor
character needs exhaustive biography. Course-level learner comments reviewed were
positive but are satisfaction reports, not independent validation. Revisit when
audience or narrative form changes.

## S2 — OpenLearn: suspense

[The Open University, suspense](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-3.4)
connects reader expectations to continued attention. Its broad assertion about
suspense in fiction should not exclude contemplative forms; our guide treats that
as an applicability limit, not a verified empirical dispute.

For S1/S2, [course rights information](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/history-the-arts/creative-writing/start-writing-fiction/content-section-0/)
marks Creative Commons with a noncommercial indication and excludes third-party
material from that grant. No course text or exercises are copied here. Exact
permissions for a future reused item must be checked, including acknowledgements
and linked terms; do not assume compatibility with unrestricted redistribution.
The linked Open University terms page could not be retrieved during this review;
copy/adaptation permissions beyond the visible course notice remain unresolved.

## S3 — Purdue OWL: fiction basics

[Purdue OWL, Fiction Basics](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/fiction_writing_basics/index.html)
relates character choices and meaningful event relationships. Useful introductory
craft instruction; conventional plot assumptions need adaptation for experimental
work. No public discussion was visible on the reviewed page. The advice is not
experimental evidence or a universal artistic ranking.

## S4 — Purdue OWL: theme and viewpoint

[Purdue OWL, Fiction Writing Basics 2](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/fiction_writing_basics/fiction_writing_basics_2.html)
discusses theme, conflict, resolution, and viewpoint. We retain deliberate control
of perspective and meaning, without adopting its generalizations about contemporary
fiction or treating traditional resolution as mandatory. No comment review was
available on that page.

For S3/S4, the pages state all rights reserved and restrict republication/adaptation.
Use as conceptual reference only here. Their [fair-use terms](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/about_the_owl/owl_information/fair_use_policy.html)
were reviewed: attributed direct links are permitted, while commercial reuse and
modified redistribution of materials are restricted. Any reproduction needs its
own terms assessment; educational access is not an unrestricted reuse grant.

## S5 — Brandon Sanderson: speculative rules and resolution

[Sanderson's First Law](https://www.brandonsanderson.com/blogs/blog/sandersons-first-law)
(2007) relates magical resolutions to reader understanding. A novelist's practical
framework, not a scientific law. The author explicitly allows exceptions and
contrasts hard and soft magic; the essay recounts disagreement about whether magic
needs rules. Preserve that distinction rather than demanding explanations for all
wonder. No reusable-content grant was established; no prose/examples copied.

## S6 — Justin Alexander: player agency

[Don't Prep Plots](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4147/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots)
(2009) advocates preparing situations for RPG play. Useful practitioner reasoning
about unexpected choices; it does not govern fixed prose. Article and comment
thread were inspected: discussion includes preparation effort and adaptation to
player habits, while the article itself cautions about abrupt style changes.
Comments are anecdotal. No reusable-content grant was established; no scenarios
or examples copied. Revisit for groups that prefer guided play.

## S7 — inkle: interactive narrative tooling

[ink overview](https://www.inklestudios.com/ink/) and
[interactive fiction tutorial](https://www.inklestudios.com/ink/web-tutorial/)
are primary tool-author material on branching narratives and previewing play.
They support trying paths during development; our coverage checks are an
engineering inference, not a promise of narrative quality. The overview identifies
an MIT-licensed tool, which does not automatically license all website content.
No tool, runtime, code sample, or binary was added. Recheck version, repository
license, and notices if a project selects ink; the workflow does not require it.

## Limits and review triggers

This is a selected starting set, not an exhaustive survey across cultures, genres,
ages, or accessibility needs. Obtain project-specific expertise and reader/playtest
feedback when those matter. Revisit on reader evidence contradicting a default,
new work forms, broken sources, changed terms, or a proposal to reuse source assets.
