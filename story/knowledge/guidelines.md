# Story Craft Guidelines

Status: advisory synthesis, checked 2026-11-14. Source keys resolve in
[sources.md](sources.md). The questions and exception examples below are WoW's
editorial synthesis, not claims that a source prescribes a universal rubric.
Use relevant defaults unless there is a reason to depart; deliberate departures
can improve a story. None is a hard requirement.

## Character and narrative coherence

| Default | Validation question | Plausible exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Give important characters recognizable motives and particular responses (S1, S3). | Can a reader explain a consequential choice from the character's situation? | An opaque or contradictory person can sustain mystery; reveal enough for the intended effect. |
| Make event relationships meaningful, not just sequential (S3). | What changed because of this event, decision, or discovery? | A vignette or associative narrative may organize experience through imagery rather than causality. |
| Select detail that establishes character and situation (S1, S3). | Does this detail deepen understanding, atmosphere, or anticipation? | Deliberate abundance can create voice or immersion; reader response decides its value. |
| Let decisions and consequences carry thematic meaning (S4). | What interpretations does the work invite beyond its topic? | Ambiguous or playful work need not deliver a thesis or moral. |

## Reader experience

| Default | Validation question | Plausible exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Give readers a reason to continue and manage expectations (S2). | What does the reader want to discover or experience next? | Contemplative work can sustain attention through voice or recognition rather than suspense. |
| Choose viewpoint and access to information deliberately (S4). | Can readers distinguish what the narrator knows from what the world establishes? | Unreliable narration or shifting perspectives can intentionally unsettle; test whether confusion serves the goal. |
| Establish enough of a speculative mechanism before relying on it to resolve trouble (S5). | Does a resolution feel supported by what readers could understand? | Wonder, myth, or soft magic may preserve mystery rather than explain mechanisms. |
| Use scene, summary, dialogue, and description according to their job (S1, S3). | Is the reader close enough to important moments without unnecessary delay? | Summary can preserve pace; extended description can itself be the experience. |

## Interactive and tabletop stories

| Default | Validation question | Plausible exception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prepare situations and responsive actors rather than mandatory player decisions (S6). | Can an unexpected but reasonable choice still lead to playable consequences? | A group may knowingly choose a tightly guided scenario; agree on the experience. |
| Check choice paths and persistent state (S7; WoW assurance inference). | Do choices, revisits, and endings behave coherently, including unusual paths? | Converging branches are useful when prior choices retain appropriate consequences. |
| Make rules and scenario information usable at the table (WoW synthesis). | Can participants find and apply a rule during play without author explanation? | Deliberately hidden rules can support discovery if that is part of the agreed experience. |

## Revision and exceptions

Revise structure and intended experience before polishing individual sentences.
Check continuity separately from interpretation: a timeline error differs from an
intentionally unreliable account. Read aloud where rhythm or dialogue matters.
These are proposed editorial methods, not measured guarantees of quality.

Ask representative readers where attention, comprehension, emotion, or agency
changed, and ask for examples. Separate observed reactions from proposed fixes.
Preserve disagreements instead of averaging them into a spurious quality score.
When readers are unavailable, record agent review as provisional.

Record a significant exception beside the affected outline or editorial note:
which guideline, intended benefit, possible cost, and how to judge the result.
Do not require bureaucracy for every sentence. Keep the departure when it serves
the intended audience; revise it when evidence shows an accidental failure.
